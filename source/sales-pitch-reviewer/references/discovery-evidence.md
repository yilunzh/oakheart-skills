# Search and AI discovery evidence

Inspect the official offer, relevant external listings, business identity and technical foundations before claiming poor visibility. Verify current provider guidance. A platform may already supply structured data and a sitemap; crawler names in robots.txt do not alone establish blocking.

Record exact queries, date, product/tool, controlled locale/device settings and returned URLs. Report presence within the sample. Do not convert result order into Google rank, absence into universal invisibility, or snippets into verified review counts, profile ownership, traffic or revenue.

Distinguish retrieval of sources, factual accuracy, citations in generated answers, recommendation frequency and attributable business outcomes. A search-tool lookup is not a cross-product AI benchmark.

Explain the benefit as making the offer easier to find, compare and cite. Connect it to accessible program/product facts, stable links, consistent identity and appropriate crawler access. Avoid guaranteed rankings or unsupported claims about special AI files. Separate search access from training preferences and autonomous transactions from research.

For measurement, use fixed customer prompts, named products, fresh sessions and repeated runs. Record mentions, citations and factual errors separately; pair them with attributable visits, inquiries and sales while stating attribution limits. Do not invent market share or a universal visibility score from a small sample.
